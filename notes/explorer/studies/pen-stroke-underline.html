1 · Single marker

recommended default

One pass of the real stroke, drawn left→right. This is the comp's own artwork moving — no invention. Restrained enough to sit under every hero on the site without wearing out.

Make your day great
0.72spower2.outdelay 0.25sstroke-width 15 linecap round1 path

Why this one. The draw reads as a pen because the ink arrives along the stroke's own length — and the round cap grows correctly at the leading edge, which a clip-wipe can't do. Cost over the static SVG is one tween. preserveAspectRatio="none" + vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke" lets one path stretch to any headline width while the stroke stays exactly 15px.

2 · Rough marker

feTurbulence displacement

Same draw, run through a displacement map so the edge frays like ink bleeding into paper. feTurbulence generates fractal noise, feDisplacementMap pushes the stroke's pixels around by it.

Stay stoked
scale 6 (subtle)baseFrequency .035 / .163 octavesseed fixed
Stay stoked
scale 11 (heavy)baseFrequency .05 / .224 octaves

Two real costs. A displacement filter forces the stroke onto its own raster surface — it re-rasterises every frame of the draw, and at hero width that is the most expensive thing on this page. Second: the filter region has to be generous (y:-120% height:340%) or the noise clips the stroke's own edge. Never animate the seed — per-frame turbulence regeneration is a jank machine. Fixed seed, animate only the draw. Heavy (scale 11) starts to read as a different, scratchier brand than the comps.

3 · Two-pass emphasis

staggered

Draw the stroke, then go over it again — second pass slightly shorter, lifted, and rotated a hair, starting before the first finishes. The gesture of underlining something twice because you mean it.

Do it right
pass 1 · 0.72s · w15pass 2 · 0.62s · w11 · α.82 stagger 0.16soffset +34 / +12rotate −0.5°

Where it earns its place. Not on every hero — it's louder than the page needs five times over. Save it for one deliberate moment (a single CTA or stat). The second pass must be thinner and dimmer, not a clone: two identical strokes read as a rendering bug, not emphasis.

4 · Scribble-circle

callout

Loops a word ~1.6 times, overshooting the start the way a hand does. Longest draw of the four because the eye has to follow it around.

Rad
1.15spower1.inOut~1.6 loopsstroke-width 9

Flagging honestly: this shape is mine, not the comps'. I searched every SVG in website/mocks/ — there is no scribble-circle anywhere in the design. The nearest marker artwork is the hand-drawn numerals in brg_community-stats.svg, which are variable-width brush glyphs, not a uniform stroke. So this is drawn in the marker idiom to show the motion; if we ship it, the shape should come from Sean's hand, not from me. Thinner stroke (9 vs 15) on purpose — at 15 a loop this tight closes up into a blob.

Home, Our Restaurants and Community are the same stroke — identical curve to within ~2 units, just different lengths. Team and Careers are the only genuinely different shapes. 6 · Nav active item

in situ

The 5px nav stroke on the white bar. Draw is fast (0.34s) — nav feedback has to feel immediate, not performed.

Home Our Restaurants Team Community Careers

Hover is a different problem from scroll-in. A draw on every hover is too slow and too chatty. Recommendation: draw once for the active item on load; keep hover as the existing fast CSS wipe. Motion should mark where you are, not narrate where your cursor is.

7 · Against what ships today

A/B

Top: current .brgw-uline — a CSS blob, scaleX(0→1). Bottom: variant 1.

now — scaleX blob
proposed — marker draw

The blob doesn't just look flatter — it deforms. scaleX squashes the shape horizontally at every frame, so its ends are ellipses for the whole transition and only resolve at the last frame. The draw never distorts the artwork: the ink simply hasn't arrived yet.
