A · Hero underline — the mechanism

A2 and A4 were broken, not slow — my bug. I had pathLength in a CSS block, and it is an SVG attribute; as CSS it is silently ignored, so the dash was one user-unit long against a path thousands of units long and nothing appeared to move. Fixed — they animate now, so the A1-vs-A2 comparison is finally a fair one. A3 is gone — you asked twice what made it different from A1, and the honest answer is nothing. Same wipe, same code; only the artwork and the words changed. A5 and A6 make that point better, so A3 was noise.

B · Hero underline — timing · now testable

S-B1 is the important card in this section. It fires only when the card is fully on screen — Salient's bottom-in-view — where B1 uses our current 16%-visible trigger. Scroll slowly past both. That difference, not the stroke, is what made ours feel like it started too early.

Placement is now measured, not guessed. You said it was wrong in all of them — it was: the bar had a hardcoded width that had nothing to do with the words above it. It now takes the width of the last line of the headline, like everything else. The timing dial is new — drag it, hit Replay, and the offset rides along in your summary so you can hand me a number rather than a description.

C · Highlight box — order · rebuilt

S-C1 and S-C2 are Salient's mechanism — a background wipe behind the words rather than a box scaled up under them. It survives a wrapped line, which ours does not.

Back to simultaneous, as you asked — the box and its words arrive together, after the text before them. C5 is genuinely fixed this time, and the cause is in its card: my clipping mask collapsed the box height whenever the mark was the whole line, so you were seeing a sliver rather than the bar.

E · As it would ship — one renderer, driven by attributes

Selected on reliability, since both read correctly. E1 (underline, wipe) has one moving part against the draw's six, uses your artwork directly rather than a derived copy, and produced zero defects this session against the draw's four. E4 (highlight, wipe) wins on one specific thing: our box is an absolutely-positioned pseudo-element, so on a wrapped heading it draws a single rectangle across both lines and the gap between them — a background paints per line instead. Headings are ACF-editable, so wrapping is not hypothetical.

The winners, expressed as the shortcode would emit them. Every card below is the same renderer reading data-* attributes — no per-variant classes, no bespoke markup. The attribute line under each is what a section author would actually write. Change a value and only that value changes, which is the whole point of the contract in SPEC-009 §0.5.
